Why an operating system, and why now

The stack is a bet about where the hard part of AI moved: from the demo to the production run, and from one vendor to five.

Why now

Three things changed at once

Agents turned one user action into many model calls, so who holds the keys, what each key may spend and what happens when a provider degrades became production questions rather than operational details.

Open-weight models became good enough for narrow, repeated tasks, so the data a team owns and the GPUs it already rents started to matter more than which vendor it calls. And procurement caught up: a legal review now asks where prompts go before an engineer asks whether the model works.

The bet

These layers belong together, the way Unix's did

The file system, the data, the models, the gateway and the agents are bought today from five vendors under five contracts, with scripts for glue. Runix draws them once and builds each as a product a team can adopt on its own.

The company that keeps the layers honest with each other, under one contract, one call interface and the customer's own storage and compute, earns the position an operating system has: everything above it can change without touching anything beneath.

How we are doing it

In the order the evidence allows

The gateway is running traffic. The file system, the data and the models are in early access. The agents are in development. Nothing on this site is marked further along than it is, and a build check fails the site if any page disagrees with the product list.

What is in writing is linked, not promised: data terms, contracting, the price catalogue and the roadmap. Three of the tools we built to run a gateway are open source, including a benchmark you can run against us.

How the layers connect

Two paths run through the stack. Requests go down through one endpoint; data comes up into training. Each product hands off to its neighbours, and each works on its own.

The request path, top down

Runix Code · Comic · your agents→Runix Router→Hosted providersorA model tuned by Runix Models

One OpenAI-compatible API in front of every model. Keys, quotas and failover sit beneath it, so the application never learns which provider answered, or whether the answer came from a model of your own.

Explore Runix Router →

The data path, bottom up

Your bucket · Runix FS→Runix Pipeline→Runix Data→Runix Models

Raw material is read through the file system, cleaned in stages you can inspect, given each domain's rules, and lands as training and evaluation sets for a model tuned to your task and served on dedicated capacity.

Explore Runix Data →

What compounds is not your data: content sent through Router is never used to train anything. What compounds is the contract, the interface and the path. One MSA and DPA cover every layer. A model tuned by Runix Models answers at the same endpoint as the hosted providers, so moving a narrow, high-volume task to a model of your own is a routing change rather than a migration. And the bucket Runix FS mounts is the one Runix Data reads from and Runix Models trains on.

Seven products, one stack

Each product sits in one layer of the stack and works on its own. The statuses are literal: Router, Models, Data and FS are in early access; Pipeline, Code and Comic are in development. Early access means a team can be onboarded today, and the qualifier on each tile says what that looks like: a key for Router, a deployment for FS, a scoped engagement for Data and Models. In development means it cannot be bought yet.

What we put in writing

Two things stall AI rollouts: the provider that fails at 3am, and the review that never ends. The products handle the first. This is what we put in writing for the second.

You own your content

Prompts, outputs and files you send through Runix are yours. We do not use your content to train models, other than a model you ask Runix Models to train on it, and we do not sell it.

Content vs. metadata

We keep operational metadata, such as usage counts and error codes, to run billing, reliability and support. The Privacy Policy says what is kept and for how long.

Paperwork on request

MSA and DPA reviewed per engagement. Refund, cancellation and acceptable-use policies are published in full.

Stated plainly

We do not claim certifications we have not earned. The Security page lists the controls that are actually in place.
